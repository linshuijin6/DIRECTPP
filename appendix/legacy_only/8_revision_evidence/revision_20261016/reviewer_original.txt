REVIEWER EVALUATION REPORT
Manuscript: “Precision Synthesis of Multi-Tracer PET via VLM-Modulated Rectified Flow for MCI Stratification”
Manuscript No.: BMEF-D-26-00169  |  Article Type: Research Article
Overall assessment: The manuscript presents a technically strong and clinically relevant framework with high novelty. However, several methodological and statistical issues should be addressed before the work can be considered for acceptance.

Major Reviewer Comments
1. High scientific novelty: The integration of 3D rectified flow, a domain-adapted BiomedCLIP/VLM, and multi-tracer PET synthesis is a clear methodological contribution beyond conventional MRI-to-PET translation.
2. Strong clinical motivation: Synthesizing AV45-PET and FDG-PET from routine MRI addresses important limitations related to PET cost, radiation exposure, and accessibility.
3. Cross-validation inconsistency: The Results section reports 3-fold cross-validation, whereas the preprocessing/methodology section reports a two-fold scheme. The exact protocol must be reconciled and stated consistently for each experiment.
4. Overstatement of prognostic stratification: EMCI-versus-LMCI classification is a stage-discrimination task and does not by itself establish longitudinal prognosis or future conversion to AD. The terminology should be moderated unless longitudinal conversion outcomes are analyzed.
5. Potential clinical-information leakage: Age, sex, weight, and cognitive scores are embedded in the text conditioning. Because these variables are strongly associated with diagnosis, ablation experiments excluding cognitive scores are necessary to demonstrate that Syn-PET is not simply re-encoding diagnostic metadata.
6. Subject-level splitting must be explicit: Because ADNI contains longitudinal scans, the authors should confirm that all visits from a given participant remain within a single fold to avoid subject leakage between training and testing.
7. External validation is incomplete for multi-tracer claims: OASIS-3 external evaluation includes AV45-PET but excludes FDG-PET. Therefore, external generalizability is demonstrated only partially and the multi-tracer generalization claim should be qualified.
8. Non-significance does not prove equivalence: Using p > 0.05 between real and synthetic PET as evidence of high fidelity is statistically insufficient. Equivalence testing (e.g., TOST) with clinically meaningful equivalence margins is more appropriate.
9. Multiple-comparison reporting should be clearer: FDR correction is appropriate, but the statistical family, number of tests, and adjusted p/q-values should be reported explicitly.
10. PET agreement metrics should be strengthened: SSIM, PSNR, MSE, and MAE are useful image-similarity metrics but are insufficient for biomarker agreement. ICC/CCC, Bland-Altman analysis, regional SUV bias, and calibration analyses would improve clinical validation.
11. Classification uncertainty is underreported: ACC, AUC, sensitivity, and specificity should be accompanied by fold-wise mean ± SD or 95% confidence intervals rather than isolated point estimates.
12. Claims of statistical superiority require direct tests: Statements such as “significantly outperforming” should be supported by formal paired comparisons, such as DeLong testing for AUC or paired bootstrap/McNemar tests where appropriate.
13. One-step sampling terminology requires correction: The phrase “exact one-step sampling” is too strong for a distilled end-point predictor. The manuscript should distinguish exact ODE integration from learned one-step approximation.
14. Domain-adapted VLM details are insufficient: The manuscript should report embedding dimensionality, PET image-feature extraction, prompt construction, affine-adapter training, threshold τ selection, and optimization details to ensure reproducibility.
15. Equation (1) needs stronger justification: The semantic-similarity constraint between FDG and AV45 embeddings may conflict with the stated goal of preserving inter-tracer differences. A sensitivity analysis for τ and an explicit ablation are recommended.
16. Demographic confounding needs control: ADNI groups differ significantly in age, sex, and other variables that are also used in the conditioning prompt. Matched, adjusted, or subgroup analyses are needed to quantify confounding effects.
17. Baseline adaptation and fairness should be documented: Some comparator methods originate from segmentation or MRI-to-CT synthesis. The authors should clearly describe how each baseline was adapted and confirm identical data splits, preprocessing, tuning effort, and training budgets.
18. Computational-efficiency claims need direct evidence: The manuscript reports approximately 138 GPU-hours for training, but should also report inference time per 3D volume, parameter count, FLOPs, memory use, and direct runtime comparisons with diffusion/flow baselines.
19. Reproducibility is a strength but can be improved: Public code, trained models, software versions, and hardware details are valuable. The authors should additionally provide fixed data-split files, random seeds, and a repository commit/version used for the reported experiments.
20. Clinical translation claims should be moderated: Statements regarding widespread screening, clinically actionable biomarkers, and early intervention exceed the evidence from retrospective ADNI/OASIS cohorts. Prospective, diverse, multi-center clinical validation is needed before such claims are justified.
Final Assessment

Recommendation
MAJOR REVISION
Priority revisions before acceptance: resolve the cross-validation inconsistency; rule out clinical-information leakage; revise the prognostic claims; and strengthen agreement, equivalence, and uncertainty analyses.
