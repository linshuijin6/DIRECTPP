\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
\subsubsection{Image-Quality Evaluation}
Image quality was assessed using structural similarity (SSIM), peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), mean squared error (MSE), and mean absolute error (MAE) between synthetic and real PET images. For subject-level comparisons of DIReCT$++$and key baseline models, we used fixed two-fold cross-validation with held-out predictions for each subject. Whole-volume metrics were calculated from the normalized PET images, and the results were summarized as mean$\pm$ sample standard deviation across subjects for each tracer.
To assess image quality within intracranial regions, we additionally evaluated DIReCT$++$, a clinically conditioned regression 3D U-Net, and Swin-UNETR using a predefined mask derived from the nonzero labels of the frozen SynthSeg segmentation. The mask included cortical and subcortical structures, white matter, cerebellum, brainstem, ventricular regions, and cerebrospinal fluid compartments. Mask-restricted MSE and MAE were computed over voxels within the mask, and PSNR was calculated from the masked MSE with a maximum intensity value of 1. Mask-restricted SSIM was calculated using a three-dimensional Gaussian window ($7\times7\times7$, $\sigma=1.5$), including only window centers for which the entire window was contained within the mask. The predefined quality-control exclusion yielded 1,597 FDG and 1,364 AV45 observations for this analysis.
For subject-matched model comparisons, we calculated paired differences and performed two-sided paired $t$-tests. Marginal 95\% confidence intervals were estimated using 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples stratified by held-out fold, retaining the original fold sizes. Cohen's $d_z$ was calculated as the mean paired difference divided by its sample standard deviation. Holm correction was applied separately for each model comparison across the eight tests comprising two tracers and four metrics. These analyses characterize differences among fixed trained models and do not estimate variability across independent training runs.
