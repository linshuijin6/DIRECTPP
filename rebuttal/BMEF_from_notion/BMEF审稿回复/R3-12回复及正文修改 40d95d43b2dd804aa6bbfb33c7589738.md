## 一、英文 Response（已确认）

**Response:**

We thank the reviewer for the insightful comments regarding deterministic generation, conditional regression, and uncertainty estimation.

We agree that MRI-to-PET synthesis is an ill-posed problem and that deterministic one-step generation cannot fully capture the uncertainty associated with individual molecular pathology.

To address the concern regarding conditional regression, we included a clinically conditioned 3D regression U-Net baseline using the same MRI inputs and clinical variables as DIReCT++. DIReCT++ achieved SSIM values of 0.9211 and 0.8844 for FDG and AV45, respectively, compared with 0.9003 and 0.8604 for the regression baseline. These results demonstrate improved image synthesis performance under matched conditioning settings, although they do not establish that the model captures the full conditional distribution.

Regarding one-step generation, we agree that the distilled prediction represents a learned approximation rather than exact integration of the underlying ODE. As also addressed in our response to Reviewer 2, Comment 13, we will correct this terminology and clarify the distillation procedure. Knowledge distillation begins at epoch 140, as specified in the original implementation.

We also acknowledge that the current deterministic framework does not provide calibrated subject-level uncertainty estimates. Consequently, synthetic PET images should not be interpreted as independent diagnostic evidence without appropriate clinical validation. We will explicitly acknowledge this limitation in the Discussion and identify uncertainty quantification, including ensemble-based or stochastic approaches, as a direction for future research.

We appreciate the reviewer's suggestions, which help clarify both the advantages and limitations of the proposed framework.

**Revised（合并 R3-2、R3-13 的已确认修改）**

```
Several limitations of this study present clear avenues for future work. First, the current text guidance relies on fundamental patient information and imaging knowledge. The use of diagnostic-group-conditioned simulation for some missing clinical scores may introduce diagnosis-related information into the conditioning inputs, which should be considered when interpreting downstream classification performance. Incorporating additional clinically accessible data, such as blood biomarkers~\cite{hansson2023blood}, could further enhance subject-level precision, necessitating the development of targeted cross-modal pretraining or prompt-learning strategies. Second, while DIReCT$++$ synthesizes $^{18}$F-AV-45 and $^{18}$F-FDG, extending the framework to include tau-PET imaging would provide a more comprehensive biomarker profile for sensitive screening at the preclinical stage. Third, to maximize clinical utility, future iterations could integrate downstream task guidance (e.g., diagnosis or prognosis labels) directly into the generative model, enabling controllable synthesis~\cite{wang2025flexibly,chang2025controllable} tailored for specific clinical decisions. The deterministic one-step formulation produces a single PET prediction per input without providing calibrated subject-level uncertainty estimates, which should be considered when assessing its clinical applicability. Ensemble-based or stochastic uncertainty estimation remains a direction for future research. Finally, the evaluation was conducted on well-characterized but limited research cohorts (ADNI, OASIS). The OASIS-3 external evaluation was limited to AV45-PET synthesis in CN and AD participants, with differences in age distributions between the groups, and therefore does not establish external generalizability for FDG-PET synthesis or MCI-related tasks. Prospective validation on more diverse, real-world clinical populations (including data from thick-slice or low-field MRI~\cite{sorby2024portable}) is essential to fully establish the generalizability and robustness of the framework.
```
