Objective: To evaluate DIReCT++, a domain-informed rectified flow framework for synthesizing FDG- and AV45-PET from MRI and clinical text inputs.

Impact Statement: This work evaluates multi-tracer synthesis and cross-sectional MCI stage discrimination, with explicit limits on clinical and prognostic interpretation.

Introduction: PET cost, radiation exposure and limited accessibility motivate MRI-based synthesis, but image similarity alone does not establish individual molecular validity.

Methods: A shared 3D rectified flow generator uses frozen BiomedCLIP-derived patient and tracer features for learned one-step inference. Separate analyses evaluate affine tracer adaptation, two-fold OOF generation, normalized regional uptake agreement and three-fold cross-sectional classification. ADNI includes 1,857 participants; historical OASIS-3 external evaluation is restricted to AV45 in CN/AD.

Results: Masked OOF comparisons favored Full over a clinical regression U-Net and MRI-only Swin-UNETR in all 16 predefined comparisons within their respective Holm families, although some effects were small. Other conditioning comparisons showed metric-dependent tradeoffs. In AD/CN classification, MRI+Syn-both AUC was 93.82 ± 3.21% versus MRI-only 87.02 ± 5.44%; MCI/CN did not improve, and EMCI/LMCI showed mixed endpoints. Clinical-only and MRI+Clinical AUC exceeded MRI+Syn-both. Historical regional intensities showed close agreement, without establishing clinical SUV/SUVR calibration or equivalence.

Conclusion: DIReCT++ supports further investigation of multi-tracer synthesis and cross-sectional classification. Extensive diagnosis-conditioned filling of clinical scores, source-identity limitations and mixed task effects preclude claims of independent diagnostic value, longitudinal prognosis or replacement of molecular confirmation.
