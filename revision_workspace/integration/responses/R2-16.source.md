We thank the reviewer for highlighting the potential confounding effects of demographic factors. We acknowledge that age and sex distributions differ across diagnostic groups in ADNI, making it important to further evaluate model performance across demographic subgroups.

To address this concern, we conducted additional age- and sex-stratified analyses. Subjects were stratified by age (<75 and ≥75 years) and sex (female and male), separately. Using the existing subject-level three-fold cross-validation test predictions, we compared MRI-only, MRI combined with synthetic dual-tracer PET, and MRI combined with real dual-tracer PET, along with the other imaging configurations. Paired bootstrap resampling was used to estimate 95% confidence intervals.

For AD versus CN classification, combining MRI with synthetic dual-tracer PET improved balanced accuracy across all four demographic subgroups, with gains ranging from 7.45 to 10.97 percentage points relative to MRI alone. For the more challenging EMCI versus LMCI classification, the balanced accuracy differences were also positive across all four subgroups, including a 9.64-percentage-point gain among subjects aged ≥75 years (95% CI: 1.23–18.05). In contrast, the MCI versus CN task did not show consistent improvements, suggesting that the contribution of synthetic PET may vary across classification tasks and patient populations.

We appreciate the reviewer’s suggestion, which has enabled a more comprehensive evaluation of model performance across age and sex subgroups. We will incorporate these additional results into the revised manuscript and provide a more cautious discussion of the potential influence of demographic characteristics on downstream classification and the clinical value of synthetic PET.
