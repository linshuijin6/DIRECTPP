We thank the reviewer for the valuable suggestion regarding computational efficiency. We agree that reporting training time alone is insufficient to characterize the computational cost of the proposed framework. Therefore, we conducted additional measurements of inference latency, model parameters, and GPU memory usage for 3D PET synthesis.

Using the trained DIReCT++ model on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU with FP32 precision, we evaluated inference performance for volumetric MRI inputs of size 160 × 192 × 160. After 10 warm-up iterations, we performed 50 synchronized timing repetitions for each tracer. The mean inference times were 251.015 ± 0.187 ms for FDG and 251.411 ± 0.256 ms for AV45, where the standard deviations represent repeated measurements on a fixed input. The generator contains approximately 9.93 million parameters, excluding the frozen BiomedCLIP encoders, and its peak PyTorch-allocated GPU memory was approximately 8.29 GiB.

Importantly, the shared DIReCT++ generator produces one tracer-specific PET volume per forward pass. The reported latency therefore reflects a single-tracer generation step with GPU-resident inputs and cached conditioning embeddings, excluding text encoding, image preprocessing, data transfers, and file I/O. We now clarify these measurement conditions in the revised manuscript to ensure accurate interpretation of the computational results.

Regarding FLOPs and direct runtime comparisons with diffusion- and flow-based baselines, we currently lack validated measurements covering the complete network operations under identical hardware and timing protocols. We therefore refrain from estimating speedup factors solely from sampling-step counts and instead report the directly measured efficiency metrics. 

We appreciate the reviewer’s recommendation. These additional measurements provide direct quantitative evidence of the computational requirements of DIReCT++ for single-step volumetric PET generation and allow us to present its efficiency more precisely and transparently.
