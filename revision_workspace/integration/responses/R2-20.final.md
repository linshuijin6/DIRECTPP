We appreciate the reviewer's thoughtful suggestions regarding the clinical translation of our work.

One of the primary motivations of DIReCT++ is to explore the feasibility of synthesizing multi-tracer PET images from routine MRI, potentially alleviating the challenges associated with PET acquisition costs, radiation exposure, and limited accessibility. Our evaluations on the ADNI and OASIS-3 datasets provide preliminary evidence of the framework's PET synthesis performance, while regional uptake analyses and downstream classification on ADNI further support its potential clinical relevance.

Nevertheless, translating these findings from retrospective research cohorts into clinical practice requires more comprehensive evidence. We agree with the reviewer that the current results are insufficient to support the direct application of synthetic PET in routine clinical screening or treatment decision-making. Accordingly, we will moderate statements regarding widespread screening, clinically actionable biomarkers, and early intervention to better distinguish the demonstrated findings from potential future clinical applications. We now also clarify that prospective validation in more diverse, real-world clinical populations remains necessary.
