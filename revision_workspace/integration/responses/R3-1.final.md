We thank the reviewer for the thoughtful comments regarding the potential overlap between clinical conditioning information and diagnostic labels. We acknowledge that cognitive scores are closely associated with diagnostic group membership in ADNI, and therefore agree that their roles in PET synthesis and downstream classification warrant careful consideration.

First, we have verified the exact clinical prompt composition, which includes age, sex, weight, and **five clinical assessments: CDR, MMSE, GDS, FAQ, and NPI-Q**. Logical Memory II is not included in the conditioning prompt. To further evaluate the diagnostic contribution of clinical information, we additionally conducted Clinical-only and MRI+Clinical classification experiments. The results demonstrate that clinical variables themselves provide strong diagnostic discrimination, highlighting the importance of considering their contribution when interpreting downstream classification performance using synthetic PET.

Furthermore, we performed an inference-time cognitive-score removal experiment while keeping the generator weights fixed. Removing cognitive scores resulted in only minor changes in regional errors for both FDG and AV45 PET, with the overall synthesis quality remaining relatively stable. This fixed-weight input intervention assesses inference-time sensitivity; it is not a retrained No-Scores ablation.

We would also like to clarify that the primary objective of DIReCT++ is to synthesize multi-tracer PET by integrating MRI with clinically available complementary information. Cognitive scores provide relevant context regarding patients’ cognitive status, which is particularly meaningful when studying fine-grained MCI stages such as EMCI and LMCI. We appreciate the reviewer’s important observation and will further clarify the role of clinical conditioning in the revised manuscript, while providing a more cautious interpretation of the downstream classification findings and the potential clinical value of synthetic PET.

The fixed-weight score-removal experiment used the separate E1 Full checkpoint family. It cannot remove information already learned from diagnosis-conditioned filled inputs during training. In the completed classification analysis, Clinical-only and MRI+Clinical AUC exceeded MRI+synthetic dual PET in all three tasks; we do not claim independent incremental diagnostic value of the synthetic PET over those clinical fields.
