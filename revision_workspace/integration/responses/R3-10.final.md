We thank the reviewer for the constructive comments regarding baseline selection and fairness of comparison. We agree that clarifying the input conditions and task-specific adaptations of different methods is important for an objective evaluation of DIReCT++.

First, regarding the use of clinical information, we would like to clarify that not all baseline methods exclude patient-specific clinical inputs. The original DIReCT incorporated individualized clinical conditioning, and the corresponding conditional diffusion and rectified flow implementations also employed clinical text guidance. Nevertheless, differences remain in their conditioning representations and network architectures. To provide a more directly comparable reference, we included a clinically conditioned 3D regression U-Net baseline.

This model uses the same MRI inputs, tracer identities, and eight clinical variables as DIReCT++, with identical subject splits and preprocessing protocols. DIReCT++ achieved SSIM values of 0.9211 for FDG and 0.8844 for AV45, compared with 0.9003 and 0.8604, respectively, for the clinically conditioned regression U-Net. These findings further support the image synthesis capability of DIReCT++ under matched clinical input conditions.

Second, regarding the MRI-to-PET methods cited in the Introduction but not included in the quantitative comparisons (Refs. 11–14), we further examined their publicly available resources and experimental protocols. Ref. 11 (JDAM) employs a slice-based evaluation protocol that differs from our 3D volumetric setting; Ref. 12 (Multi-view Diffusion) lacks directly reproducible public code and pretrained weights; Ref. 13 (MRI2PET) provides public code, but independent training and evaluation under our unified data splits and protocols would require additional computational resources and experimental time; and Ref. 14 (UniSyn) does not provide complete public reproduction resources and relies on different datasets and pretraining conditions. Consequently, we could not include these methods in a unified quantitative comparison. We now clarify the coverage of the baseline comparisons in the revised manuscript.

Regarding the existing baselines, we selected representative approaches spanning different medical image synthesis paradigms, including CycleGAN, SwinUNETR, diffusion models, and rectified flow. SwinUNETR was adapted for 3D PET intensity regression, while CycleGAN served as a conventional cross-modal image translation reference. SegGuidedDiff was implemented using a 2D slice-based approach with anatomical segmentation guidance. We now clarify these implementation details and the corresponding task adaptations in the revised manuscript.

Furthermore, we now revise the original statement that all baseline hyperparameters were optimized to their reported best configurations and instead describe the actual training parameters and evaluation settings.

We appreciate the reviewer’s suggestions. By incorporating a clinically matched regression baseline, clarifying the reasons for not including certain MRI-to-PET methods, and providing more precise descriptions of the existing baseline implementations, we aim to improve the transparency, fairness, and interpretability of the experimental comparisons.
