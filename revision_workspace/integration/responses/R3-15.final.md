We thank the reviewer for highlighting the importance of clarifying the methodological contributions of DIReCT++ beyond the original DIReCT. We agree that the key differences and additional contributions should be more explicitly stated.

The original DIReCT established an MRI-to-PET synthesis framework based on rectified flow and biomedical text guidance. Building upon this foundation, DIReCT++ introduces several extensions. First, it extends single-tracer synthesis to both FDG and AV45 PET through a shared generative network with tracer-specific conditioning. Second, it incorporates a lightweight affine adaptation mechanism that aligns tracer-text embeddings with PET image features while regulating cross-tracer representational similarity, providing medically informed tracer conditioning. Furthermore, the framework employs a learned one-step generation strategy and extends the evaluation to multi-tracer PET synthesis and downstream disease classification tasks.

To further investigate these developments, we conducted additional comparisons and sensitivity analyses examining shared versus separate tracer modeling, clinical conditioning strategies, and tracer representation adaptation. These results provide complementary insights into the roles of different components while also revealing metric-dependent trade-offs in image synthesis performance.

The revised manuscript distinguishes the methodological extensions introduced in DIReCT++ from those established in DIReCT and interprets their contributions without attributing the overall performance differences to any single component.
