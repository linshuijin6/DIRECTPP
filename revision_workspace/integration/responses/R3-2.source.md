We thank the reviewer for the detailed comments regarding the distribution of clinical assessments in Table S1 and the handling of missing values.

Upon reviewing our clinical data preprocessing procedure, we confirmed that some clinical assessment scores were missing from the original dataset. During data preparation, missing scores were simulated based on diagnostic-group information to provide complete clinical conditioning inputs for the model.

We acknowledge that, although this procedure enabled the completion of missing input fields, it may have affected the within-group distributions of clinical scores and indirectly introduced diagnostic-group information into the imputed values. Therefore, the relatively small standard deviations observed for certain clinical assessments in the original Table S1 should not be interpreted as reflecting the natural variability of actual clinical observations. We will examine the missingness of individual variables across diagnostic groups using the available original clinical records and revise the presentation and interpretation of the corresponding statistics in Table S1, explicitly distinguishing observed from simulated values. Where historical missingness information cannot be reliably recovered from the available records, we will acknowledge this limitation rather than make unsupported assumptions.

Regarding the reviewer’s concern about potential circularity involving diagnostic information, we agree that it is important to distinguish the contribution of clinical conditioning to PET synthesis from the potential influence of diagnosis-related information on downstream classification. In our previously conducted inference-time sensitivity analysis, removing cognitive scores while keeping the trained generator weights fixed resulted in only minor changes in PET synthesis errors. This finding suggests that image generation does not depend entirely on cognitive-score conditioning. Nevertheless, this analysis alone cannot fully exclude the potential effects of the original missing-value completion strategy.

Accordingly, based on the existing experimental results and cognitive-score sensitivity analysis, we will more clearly delineate the potential implications of diagnostic-group-conditioned completion for the interpretation of PET synthesis and downstream classification results.

We appreciate the reviewer for identifying this important data-processing issue. By clarifying the sources of clinical data, the missing-value handling strategy, and its implications for interpreting the results, we aim to improve the transparency and reliability of our study.
