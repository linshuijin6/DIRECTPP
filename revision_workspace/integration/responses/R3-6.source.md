We thank the reviewer for the careful assessment of our data splitting strategy, potential information leakage, and image preprocessing procedures. We appreciate the opportunity to clarify these important methodological details.

First, PET synthesis and downstream classification employed two distinct cross-validation protocols. Specifically, PET synthesis was conducted using subject-level two-fold cross-validation. In each fold, the generative model was trained on one subset and used to synthesize PET images for the held-out subjects. The synthetic images from both held-out folds were then combined to form an out-of-fold (OOF) dataset covering the study cohort. Importantly, we confirm that the synthetic PET images used for downstream classification were obtained from these original OOF predictions, ensuring that each subject’s synthetic PET was generated by a model that had not been trained on that subject. Downstream classification was subsequently performed using an independent three-fold cross-validation scheme. Thus, the two-fold and three-fold protocols correspond to different experimental stages and do not require identical or nested fold assignments.

Second, although ADNI is a longitudinal study, our analysis retained only one imaging record per participant, preventing repeated visits from the same subject from being assigned to different folds. We further verified the subject-level independence of the training and held-out subsets in the two-fold PET generation experiments, as well as the training, validation, and test partitions in the updated three-fold classification experiments. No subject-level overlap was identified within these respective partitions.

Regarding the classification cohorts, the updated experiments included 470 subjects for AD versus CN (165 AD and 305 CN), 741 subjects for MCI versus CN (436 MCI and 305 CN), and 436 subjects for EMCI versus LMCI (287 EMCI and 149 LMCI). The MCI group in these classification experiments comprised subjects explicitly categorized as EMCI or LMCI, while MCI-U subjects were excluded. We will clarify these task-specific sample sizes and selection criteria in the revised manuscript.

Regarding image acquisition and tracer selection, the PET modalities used in this study were AV45 (florbetapir) and FDG, obtained from ADNI, without the inclusion of florbetaben PET. MRI and PET images were paired according to subject identity during dataset preparation. However, the detailed historical records of acquisition-date matching were not fully retained, preventing a reliable reconstruction of the maximum MRI–PET acquisition interval. We therefore refrain from specifying an unverified numerical threshold and acknowledge this limitation in describing the original data selection procedure.

Finally, we appreciate the reviewer’s question regarding PET preprocessing and potential multi-scanner variability. We would like to clarify that our study utilized preprocessed PET images downloaded from the official ADNI repository rather than raw PET data. Building upon these preprocessed images, we implemented a standardized cross-modal preprocessing pipeline, as described in Section 4.3. Specifically, T1-weighted MRI scans underwent skull stripping and histogram matching, followed by rigid registration of each PET image to its corresponding MRI. Both modalities were then affinely normalized to the MNI152 standard space using SPM, with consistent spatial transformations to preserve cross-modal alignment. All images were subsequently standardized to a common spatial dimension of 160 × 192 × 160 voxels at 1-mm isotropic resolution and intensity-normalized to the [0, 1] range using min–max scaling.

These procedures were intended to establish consistent spatial representations and input intensity ranges across subjects and imaging modalities. The use of ADNI-provided preprocessed PET images also helped reduce inconsistencies associated with heterogeneous initial image processing. Nevertheless, we recognize that spatial alignment and intensity normalization do not necessarily eliminate scanner-dependent differences in spatial resolution, reconstruction characteristics, or quantitative response. Accordingly, we will clarify the scope of the preprocessing procedures applied in this study without interpreting them as comprehensive scanner-specific harmonization.
