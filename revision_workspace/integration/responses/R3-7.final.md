We thank the reviewer for the constructive suggestions regarding classification performance reporting, statistical evaluation, and the reliability of the MRI-only baseline.

To address these concerns, we re-evaluated the imaging input configurations using subject-level three-fold cross-validation with a consistent DenseNet121 architecture and training protocol. We now report the fold-wise mean and standard deviation, together with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals, for accuracy (ACC), area under the ROC curve (AUC), sensitivity (SEN), specificity (SPE), and balanced accuracy. We also provide paired bootstrap comparisons, fold-wise DeLong tests, and ROC curves with confidence bands.

For the MCI versus CN task highlighted by the reviewer, the MRI-only baseline achieved an ACC of 67.48% ± 0.23% and an AUC of 71.59% ± 1.80% in the updated evaluation, providing a more reliable reference for comparing different imaging inputs.

To account for class imbalance in the EMCI versus LMCI task, we additionally report balanced accuracy. Compared with MRI alone, combining MRI with synthetic dual-tracer PET yielded a 6.91-percentage-point improvement in balanced accuracy (95% CI: 2.02–11.98), accompanied by improved sensitivity for the minority LMCI class, although specificity was lower. This finding suggests that synthetic PET may provide useful information for the challenging task of fine-grained MCI stage discrimination.

We have revised the classification results and their interpretation accordingly to more accurately characterize the performance of different imaging inputs across the three tasks.
