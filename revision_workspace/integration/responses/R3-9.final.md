We thank the reviewer for the constructive suggestion to strengthen the ablation study. We agree that distinguishing the contributions of text guidance, clinical conditioning, tracer representation adaptation, and sampling strategy is important for a more comprehensive assessment of DIReCT++.

We revisited the existing comparisons and supplemented the analyses of key conditioning components. Because the components serve different functions, we distinguish comparisons involving independently trained models from interventions applied only during inference, rather than treating them as interchangeable forms of ablation.

First, regarding text conditioning, the existing 3D rectified flow (3D RF) baseline does not use the BiomedCLIP-enhanced text guidance. However, it also differs from DIReCT++ in other implementation aspects; hence, the performance difference cannot be attributed exclusively to the text guidance module. Fixed-weight patient-conditioning interventions, including empty or mismatched patient features, demonstrate output sensitivity to the supplied context. They are not a retrained text-free model or a complete no-text ablation; a nonzero output response alone does not establish clinical benefit.

Second, to evaluate the independent contribution of patient-specific clinical information, we conducted a training-level comparison between the Full and Only-tracer configurations, which retains tracer guidance while excluding the patient clinical condition. We also added a training comparison between BiomedCLIP-based text encoding and an encoder for structured clinical variables to assess the contribution of the VLM representation relative to conventional numerical conditioning. These comparisons address the clinical-conditioning and encoder-design aspects of the requested ablations.

Regarding a clinical-prompt-only configuration, the tracer token in our shared multi-tracer framework not only carries tracer-related information but also specifies the target PET modality. Removing it entirely while retaining only the patient clinical information would leave the network without an explicit distinction between AV45 and FDG generation targets. Therefore, this configuration would not represent a well-defined dual-tracer comparison under the current architecture and task definition.

For one-step versus multi-step ODE sampling, the available validated measurements cover single-step generation only; no matched multi-step quality/latency comparison is claimed. The recovered training code fixes t=0 from epoch 140 and fits the PET-minus-MRI endpoint residual without a separately recovered teacher trajectory. For joint versus separate tracer generation, the original manuscript already includes a single-tracer DIReCT comparison. We now clarify that this evaluates the overall difference between separately trained single-tracer models and the shared multi-tracer framework, rather than isolating a synergistic benefit from joint training alone.

Finally, the contribution of frozen versus adapted tracer embeddings is discussed together with the analysis of tracer representation adaptation. We now avoid attributing a performance gain solely to the adaptation module without an appropriately controlled comparison.

The historical estimate of approximately 138 GPU-hours was not independently verified. The revised computational description therefore uses measured single-step forward latency, parameter count and memory, and does not infer a speedup from step counts or claim a completed equal-protocol FLOP/runtime ranking. We distinguish independently trained model comparisons from fixed-weight inference interventions and feature-level adapter analyses. We now distinguish the purposes and evidential limitations of these experiments in the revised manuscript, without interpreting a baseline comparison as a controlled single-component ablation.
