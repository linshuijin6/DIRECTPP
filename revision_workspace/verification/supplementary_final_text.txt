Supplementary Methods: conditioning prompts and adapter analysis
The following is the historical clinical prompt template, with bracketed field placeholders instead of
participant identifiers or invented measurements. Values use the historical formatting (rounded age
and scores, one decimal for weight and CDR). Its use does not validate the filled values as clinical
observations.

     Subject is a [age]-year-old [sex] with a weight of [weight] kg. The global Clinical Dementia
     Rating (CDR) score, which assesses dementia severity (0: no dementia to 3: severe dementia),
     is [global_cdr]. The Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score, assessing cognitive func-
     tion (0: severe impairment to 30: normal), is [mmse_score]. The Geriatric Depression Scale
     (GDS) score, screening depression (0: no depression to 15: severe depression), is [gds_score].

FDG tracer prompt.

     FDG PET is a functional brain imaging technique that visualizes the dynamic changes in glucose
     metabolism, directly linked to neuronal energy demands and synaptic activity. It serves as a
     tool to assess functional connectivity and energy utilization across brain regions. Areas with
     decreased metabolic activity, such as those affected by neurodegenerative diseases, should exhibit
     reduced signal intensity. High-intensity metabolic hotspots in gray matter (e.g., the cerebral
     cortex and basal ganglia) are key markers of neuronal activity.

AV45 tracer prompt.

     AV45 PET is a molecular imaging technique that highlights the static distribution of amyloid-
     beta plaques, a critical pathological marker of Alzheimer’s disease. This imaging modality
     provides a spatial map of amyloid deposition in cortical regions (e.g., the temporal, parietal, and
     frontal lobes) and can distinguish amyloid-positive areas from amyloid-negative white matter
     regions. The primary focus is on identifying amyloid deposition patterns to assess disease
     progression and pathological burden.

The tracer texts are fixed model inputs; their statements about pathology or progression are not
evidence that synthetic PET establishes a biological diagnosis or predicts progression.
